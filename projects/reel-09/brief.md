# reel-09 brief: Bicep curl, elbow check

- Purpose: teach that the elbow should be relaxed, not pinned to the ribs, on a standing one-arm dumbbell curl.
- Platform: Telegram on a phone, often muted. 1080x1920, 30 fps, 15 s, MP4. **No sound at all** (user said so).
- Look: flat vector athlete drawn in code (`work/fig.py`), warm off-white background, Inter ExtraBold text,
  red #DE3434 = current, green #1EA054 = correct. Max 6 words per line. No logos.
- Camera: three-quarter front view (55 deg from side). A pure side view hides the elbow-to-ribs gap (it points at
  the camera), so the curling arm sits on the outside of the body outline where the gap shows as clear space.

| Time | Scene | Motion | Text |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | Title | dumbbell icon, slow rise | Bicep curl: / elbow check |
| 2-6 | CURRENT (red pill) | elbow pulled in onto the ribs, slightly back, shoulder raised (trap line), 3-step hitchy lift 1.6 s, 2-step drop 1.3 s, small tremor; red glow + ring on the contact spot | Elbow locked / to the side |
| 6-11 | CORRECT (green pill) | small gap, elbow drifts 6 deg forward and a little out on the way up, cosine 2 s up / 2 s down; dashed green arc of the dumbbell path | Elbow free, / smooth and natural |
| 11-15 | Split: current top (red tint), correct bottom (green tint), same start, in sync | same motions, both end at the bottom at 15 s | Don't lock the elbow. / Let it move naturally. |

Body stays still in both versions; only the elbow and the smoothness differ.
